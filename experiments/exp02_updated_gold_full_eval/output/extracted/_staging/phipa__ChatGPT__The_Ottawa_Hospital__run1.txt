Executive performance-management readout
For The Ottawa Hospital, the period is notable less for privacy-breach publicity than for patient-access and electronic-record governance issues. The two identified IPC matters both relate to how TOH administers rights over information held in its Epic/electronic record environment: record correction and statements of disagreement in Decision 326, and the form in which electronic records must be provided in Decision 340. The IPC did not find a substantive PHIPA breach in either matter. Decision 340 is particularly relevant to digital-health governance because it confirms that a custodian's access obligation does not necessarily require it to generate a new technical representation of information—such as a screenshot—that it does not create or maintain. The Ottawa Hospital | PHIPA Decision 326 — correction of an after-visit summary / statement of disagreement | A patient asked The Ottawa Hospital to correct portions of an after-visit summary under PHIPA and subsequently requested that a statement of disagreement be attached to the record under s. 55(13). The hospital declined to make the requested corrections because it was not required to do so under the Act, but added the statement of disagreement to the patient’s electronic health record. On January 26, 2026, the Ontario IPC upheld the hospital’s position, finding that the hospital had no duty to make the requested corrections and had satisfied its obligation by attaching the disagreement statement to the electronic record. The adjudicator also found that the hospital was not required to make the statement accessible through the patient portal and dismissed the complaint. (IPC Ontario) | January 26, 2026 | Information and Privacy Commissioner of Ontario — PHIPA Decision 326, Complaint HA23-00094 | IPC PHIPA Decision 326
The Ottawa Hospital | PHIPA Decision 340 — access to electronic medical-record display formats/screenshots | A patient requested “samples” or screenshots of 25 electronic medical-record displays, including items such as “Storyboard” and “Snapshot,” from The Ottawa Hospital’s EMR. The hospital offered printed copies of the personal health information that would be displayed but declined to produce screenshots. In PHIPA Decision 340, dated June 25, 2026, the IPC found that the requested screenshots could potentially qualify as “records” under PHIPA, but the hospital did not create or maintain the screenshots and therefore was not required to create them in response to the access request. The adjudicator concluded that the hospital had proposed reasonable alternatives for providing access to the underlying information and dismissed the complaint, along with additional allegations concerning PHIPA offences, the hospital’s response to an earlier access request, and procedural fairness.
