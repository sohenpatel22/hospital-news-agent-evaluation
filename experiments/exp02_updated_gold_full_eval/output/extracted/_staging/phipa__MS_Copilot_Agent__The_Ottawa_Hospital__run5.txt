The Ottawa Hospital | Access to Epic EMR displays and screenshots, PHIPA Decision 340 | A patient requested samples or screenshots of 25 display items in The Ottawa Hospital’s Epic electronic medical record, including “Storyboard” and “Snapshot.” The IPC found that screenshots could potentially qualify as records under PHIPA, contrary to the hospital’s categorical position, but concluded that the hospital did not create or maintain the requested screenshots and was not required to create them. The IPC also found that the hospital had proposed reasonable alternatives, including printed copies of the personal health information displayed by the relevant Epic functions. The adjudicator dismissed the access complaint and the complainant’s additional allegations. For performance management, the decision is relevant to how hospitals distinguish information displayed dynamically in an EMR from records actually maintained by the custodian, and how reasonable alternatives may satisfy an access request where the requested artifact does not exist. The Ottawa Hospital, Civic Campus | Access to records concerning a patient’s care, interaction between PHIPA and FIPPA, Order PO-4820 | A substitute decision-maker sought emails, notes and meeting records relating to his son’s care. The IPC found that the records contained the son’s personal health information. It determined that the draft meeting minutes were dedicated primarily to that information, while the emails were primarily administrative or communicative records. The IPC then applied PHIPA’s FIPPA “flow-through” provision to the remaining access questions. It upheld the hospital’s reliance on applicable exclusions and exemptions, including for certain labour-relations or employment-related material and advice or recommendations. However, the IPC found that the hospital had not conducted a reasonable search and ordered a further search for responsive records. This is operationally significant because it illustrates both the record-by-record assessment required when PHIPA and FIPPA overlap and the need for a demonstrably reasonable search even when withholding decisions are otherwise upheld.
